## Rules every group follows

1. **Per-site integration.** Each site's signal is integrated from the spectrum against that site's channel response when
   the site is read, on every render path. A cache may hold geometry, material indices, kernels and spectral radiance at
   the nodes. No cache or table holds a value in which a channel response has been summed over the spectrum, and nothing
   is keyed by channel, material or signal level. Each design below says what its caches hold.
2. **One flag.** A speed-against-accuracy choice is the one `fast` flag (docs/API_STYLE.md, "Exact and fast modes"). The
   default is the faster method for `"srgb-8"`, `"srgb-16"` and the display view, and the full method for `"dng"`,
   `"jpeg"`, `"png"`, `simulateIdealReference` and `fast: false`. Every fast method has an entry in
   `SIMULATE_CAPTURE_FAST_METHODS`, and its full method is built and tested in the same group.
3. **One path where the results are the same.** A faster method that equals the full one within the equivalence tolerance
   (each site's expected electrons within 1e-9 relative, 8-bit samples within one code; `test/helpers/view-equivalence.ts`)
   replaces it: no flag, no entry. A hash lock may be re-recorded for such a change only with the equivalence helper
   passing on that case, and the change is listed in the changelog's migration list.
4. **Regions and parts** equal the same pixels of the whole frame bit for bit on the same path. Results are deterministic
   (fixed tiles at absolute positions, seeded draws).
5. **Size limits are the owner's.** No group raises a limit of `test/capture-bundle.test.ts`, `test/framing-bundle.test.ts`
   or `site/checks/budgets.json`. New code loads on demand in its feature's own chunk. A group that cannot fit stops and
   reports its measured bytes. Every group reports the four worker rows before and after its change.
6. **Evidence.** Every model has a cited free source, every speed claim a before/after measurement with its command, and
   every approximation is labeled.
7. **Public repository.** Plain, neutral wording and US spelling (quoted names keep theirs). Nothing about private
   deployments or downstream applications beyond "a downstream app".
8. **Tests run once per tree and environment.** A group runs the tests of the files it changed, not the whole suite; the
   whole suite runs once on the integration branch (locally, on a quiet machine) and once in CI on the release pull
   request. A new test file reports its local time under coverage so that integration can enter its shard weight.
9. **Shared files.** Only the wave's wiring group edits `src/facade/simulate-capture.ts` and
   `src/facade/simulate-display-view.ts`. Only integration edits `src/index.ts`, `src/facade/fast-methods.ts`,
   `CHANGELOG.md`'s 2.29.0 section, docs/API_STYLE.md, the generated API reference, `test/shard-weights.json` and the
   limits in the bundle tests. A group hands integration its exports, registry entries, changelog paragraph and measured
   tables in the body of its last commit ("Handoff:").

#### #749 and #750 (CPU): card-edge sums above 64 sites, and faster at large blur

One piece of work: both issues come from edge sites summing every kernel tap directly ((2R + 1)² taps, each with its own
visibility).

- **Design.** For the uniform disc, a kernel row is one run of constant weight with a partial cell at each end. For
  surface k at lattice point p and pupil row Py, the visible part is the pupil chord cut by each card's interval, a
  small set of Px intervals that `rowIntervals` gives exactly. The sum over a row is then a difference of row prefix sums
  of the surface's spectral radiance (per node) for the interior taps, plus the boundary taps with their exact partial
  cover. Cost per edge site falls from O(R²·N) to O(R·segments·N). Prefix rows live in the spectral lattice tiles
  (spectral radiance at the nodes per lattice point; no channel value). For shaped kernels (blades, spherical
  aberration, cat's eye) the weights vary across a row: `siteKernelRuns` splits the row into runs of equal weight where
  there are few, and the direct sum stays where there are many.
- **Limits.** The disc's bound rises from 64 to `DEFOCUS_DISC_KERNEL_MAXIMUM_RADIUS_SITES` (512, the single-plane
  bound). The shaped kernel's bound rises to at least 128, set by a measured work bound per edge site (#749's comment
  needs 89 at full quality). Both bounds are checked before allocation.
- **Accuracy and test.** Run sum against the direct sum at R = 3, 11, 29 and 64: within 1e-9 relative per site, samples
  within one code (rule 3: the run sum replaces the direct sum for discs). The accuracy table of
  docs/LAYERED_SCENES.md re-run at R = 96, 128 and 256 against the brute-force aperture integration: within the existing
  bounds (0.5 % of the local swing full, 2 % fast; energy 0.2 % and 1 %).
- **Speed target.** #750's case (24 MP, a 192 × 128 view part, f/1.4 near focus, radii 14 to 29): from 53–73 s to at
  most 5× the small-blur part (about 5 s); an estimate from the tap counts, reported as measured with
  `scripts/benchmark-layered-scenes.mjs` (new cases) and against the #670 set.
- **Fast and registry:** no new entry (same sum regrouped). `scene.layers/occlusion-samples` (4 or 16 rows) is unchanged.
- **Bundle:** layered chunk only. **Files:** `layered-edge-sum.ts`, `spectral-lattice-tiles.ts` (prefix rows),
  `test/layered-scene-edge-runs.test.ts`, the benchmark script. It reads `rowIntervals` and does not edit
  `layered-visibility.ts`.
- **Size and risk:** L; medium (cancellation in prefix differences at large R: use per-tile prefix origins, as
  `convolveSiteKernelRuns` does, and test the worst case).

#### #747: the surface at a frame point

- **Design.** Two pure exports, so that a caller with a rectilinear lens does not bundle the lens catalogue:
  - `calculateLayeredChartSurfaceAt({ scene, aspect, u, v })` → `{ kind: "card" | "back-plane", index, distanceM,
    framePoint: { u, v } }`: the internal `chiefSurface` lookup. The far surface has no edge, so every point has a
    surface.
  - `calculateLayeredChartSurfaceAtPixel({ scene, output: { width, height }, pixel: { x, y }, sensor, lens })` maps a
    pixel center through the request's lens (rectilinear, a `projection`, `distortion`, or a catalogue lens, after #744)
    with the engine's own mapping functions, then calls the first. It returns `null` where the pixel sees no scene
    (outside a circular fisheye's image circle or at 90° and beyond).
  - `distanceM` is the distance along the axis, the value `focus-control`'s `finite-surface` target takes.
- **Accuracy and test:** for every pixel of three requests (rectilinear, distortion, fisheye catalogue lens) the answer
  equals the surface the display view's pilot reads (`lattice.chiefSurface`); a tap-to-focus example in
  docs/LAYERED_SCENES.md compiles.
- **Fast and registry:** none. **Bundle:** a bundle test like `test/framing-bundle.test.ts`: the first export alone
  stays free of the catalogue. **Files:** new `src/facade/layered-chart-query.ts`, tests. **Size and risk:** S; low.

#### #752: in-focus mask for display views

- **Design.** View option `focusMask: true | { circleOfConfusionMm? }` on `simulateDisplayView` and
  `PreparedDisplayView.render`. Result field `view.focusMask = { width, height, circleOfConfusionMm, score: Float32Array,
  label }` for the rendered rectangle. Per display pixel: the chief ray's surface at the pixel center (#747's lookup),
  its blur circle c from `calculateDefocusCircle` at the resolved focus (the sampled one with `lens.focusScatter`), and
  `score = max(0, 1 − c / c₀)`, c₀ the circle of confusion (default: the frame's diagonal / 1500, as the depth report).
  `score > 0` is "within the depth of field" by the same limits as `scene.layers[].withinDepthOfField`. A single-plane
  scene gives one value for the whole frame. It is analytic: no image is analyzed and no render is needed, so the mask
  is the same with `fast` true or false and on the CPU and GPU routes. `label` says it is a geometric thin-lens guide
  (no diffraction, no aberrations).
- **Accuracy and test:** each surface's pixels have the score of its depth-report entry (1e-12); a region equals the
  whole view's pixels bit for bit; `score > 0` agrees with `withinDepthOfField` on the presets at three f-numbers.
- **Fast and registry:** none (one analytic method). **Bundle:** the body is in the layered chunk
  (`layered-chart-query.ts`); the view's option and the single-plane constant are the fast-row cost (estimate +100 B).
- **Files:** `layered-chart-query.ts`; wiring in `simulate-display-view.ts`, `display-view.ts` (result field only).
- **Size and risk:** S–M; low.

#### #744: distortion, vignetting and projection on layered scenes

- **Design.** The sum moves from the planar lattice to the sensor's site lattice in image space, as the single-plane
  path did in #548. A lattice point's sensor point is mapped through the lens (inverse distortion, then the projection)
  to a field direction; frame units are angles, so that direction is one frame point (u, v) on every surface. The
  radiance of surface k at lattice point p is L_k(u(p), v(p)) times the field factor F(p) (vignetting throughput ×
  distortion area factor), as the catalogue signal takes it at each tap point. Defocus and occlusion stay on the lattice:
  the ray through image point p and pupil point P meets surface k's image at p − ρ_k·P.
- **Card edges.** In image space a card's edge is a curve. Near a lattice point it is taken as its tangent line (a
  half-plane; `edges(k, p, q)` in L0's interface), so a pupil row's open part is still an exact interval. This is an
  approximation of second order in the edge's curvature over one kernel footprint; the test below measures it, and a
  footprint whose curvature term exceeds a stated share of a site falls back to splitting the footprint.
- **Fisheyes.** Directions at 90° and beyond read zero radiance and a point outside the image circle looks backward, as
  on the single-plane path.
- **The capture's provider** keys each environment query by its lattice point, as `createDefocusImageSpaceProvider`
  does, so the lens field mapping stays in the environment and the provider declares no PSF.
- **A frame scale** (1 today) is an input of the geometry, for #748's breathing.
- **Accuracy and test.** With no distortion and a rectilinear lens: bit-identical to L0's values. With distortion:
  occlusion edges against a brute-force aperture integration through the same mapping (staggered 64 × 64 pupil points):
  within the existing bounds (0.5 % full, 2 % fast). One surface filling the frame equals the single-plane lens signal
  within 1e-9. Capture against view per site within 1e-9. Vignetting: a white scene's falloff equals the profile's
  within 1e-9.
- **Fast and registry:** no new entry: one method, with its bound measured against the reference.
- **Bundle:** layered chunk. **Files:** `layered-lattice-geometry.ts`, `layered-visibility.ts` (half-plane rows; shared
  with the edge-sum group through L0's interface), `layered-scene-provider.ts`, tests.
- **Size and risk:** L; medium–high (the provider's query keying under a lens mapping; edge curvature on fisheyes).

#### #745: mixed illumination on layered scenes

- **Design.** The illumination gradient is a function of the frame point. Each surface point is lit by the blend at its
  own frame point (u, v): E(u, v, λ) = Σ_j w_j(u, v)·S_j(λ), and L_k = ρ_k(λ)·E / π, formed per lattice point at the
  spectral nodes when a tile is filled. The rule is documented as an approximation: one light field over the frame's
  angles, no shadows between surfaces and no falloff with depth. Point lights keep their own spectrum. Metering, white
  balance and the pilot read the chief ray's surface under the blend, as the single-plane mixed path does.
- **Accuracy and test.** A single-source `illumination` equals the plain illuminant bit for bit. One surface filling the
  frame equals the single-plane mixed-light signal within 1e-9. Two sources: a site on each card away from edges equals
  the hand-computed blend × reflectance integrated against the channel response within 1e-9.
- **Fast and registry:** none new; the spectral step follows `sensor.spectralStepNanometers` as on every path.
- **Bundle:** layered chunk; the blend function comes from the opt-in kit's `mixed-illumination.ts`, already loaded for
  an `illumination` request. **Files:** `layered-surface-radiance.ts`, tests. **Size and risk:** M; low–medium.

#### #741: layered scenes with a catalogue lens

- **Design.** Three parts on top of #744's lattice (the catalogue's distortion, vignetting and projection are #744).
  1. **The lens's PSF** takes the place diffraction has today: each spectral node's PSF kernel follows the layered sum
     ("applied per layer", the single-plane path's approximation), taken per field zone as the display view does
     (#569): zone-center kernels on fast requests, zones of one site on full ones.
  2. **The lens's bokeh** (blades, spherical aberration, cat's eye) shapes every surface's kernel through the existing
     layered kernel path, with the lens's own options (`resolveGenericLens(...).character.bokeh`).
  3. **Axial color as a signed blur per wavelength.** The catalogue's axial color is a defocus W_ax·a²·D(λ) in the
     wavefront. As a geometric blur it adds a(λ) = 8·N·W_ax·a²·D(λ)/pitch sites to a surface's signed blur diameter
     (the same conversion the bokeh uses for spherical aberration, b = 8·N·W040/pitch). Surface k's kernel at node n is
     the bokeh kernel for σ·c_k − b + a(λ_n): blue and red focus at different depths, so a card in front of the focus
     plane and one behind it fringe in opposite colors. The PSF that follows the sum is built without the axial-color
     term (it is in the kernel now), by an option of the catalogue PSF builder. A surface exactly at the focus distance
     convolves nothing and keeps the lens's own PSF, as the single-plane path does. Source: Wyant and Creath 1992 (free
     author copy, already cited in docs/LENS_CATALOGUE.md) for the wavefront and for the transverse ray aberration as
     the wavefront's slope, which gives the conversion above. The model is an approximation and is labeled as one.
- **Fast and exact.** Kernels per node multiply the edge sum's work by the node count. New registry entry
  `scene.layers/chromatic-kernel-nodes` (override `lens.catalogue`): fast builds each surface's kernel at the PSF
  profile's five wavelength nodes and interpolates the summed spectral radiance linearly between them, as the profile
  itself is interpolated; full builds a kernel at every spectral node. Reported in the `scene.layers` assumption
  (`chromaticKernels: { nodes, fast }`) and `diagnostics.fast.applied["lens.catalogue"]`. The existing entries
  `lens.catalogue/view-psf-zones` and `lens.catalogue/cats-eye` extend to layered requests (their `runs` and text).
- **Accuracy and test.**
  - Go/no-go first: one surface's per-node kernel (bokeh at σ·c − b + a(λ), then the PSF without axial color) against
    the lens's defocused wave PSF computed directly (`createWavefrontPupilGrid` with the surface's defocus in W020) at
    three field points, five wavelengths and defocus of ±2, ±8 and ±32 sites: report the L1 difference per node and the
    centroid of each channel's edge spread. Target: channel edge positions within 0.25 site and the fringe's sign right
    on both sides of focus. If the target fails, the group stops and reports.
  - A layered scene whose surfaces all lie at one distance against the single-plane catalogue capture at that distance:
    report the difference (it is not zero by design: the single-plane path has no per-wavelength blur; decision 4).
  - Fast against full: expected values within 0.1 code RMS and 1 code at most on the tabletop preset at three
    f-numbers; if not, the fast node count rises or the fast method is dropped.
  - Capture against view within 1e-9; regions equal the whole frame.
- **Refusals that stay:** `lens.psf`, `lens.pupilSampling`, and `lens.defocus.catsEye` with `lens.diffraction`.
- **Bundle:** layered chunk and the catalogue lens's chunk (the PSF builder's option); the facade's guard is in the
  wave-4 wiring (fast-row estimate in the table above). **Files:** new `src/facade/layered-catalogue-kernels.ts`,
  `src/equipment/` catalogue PSF builder (one option), tests, `scripts/benchmark-layered-scenes.mjs` (catalogue cases
  against #670). The kernel groups are passed in `layered-scene-setup.ts` by the wave-4 wiring.
- **Size and risk:** L; high (a new optical approximation; cost at edge sites; depends on #744 and on the edge-sum
  group's kernel groups).

#### #748: focus scatter and focus breathing on layered scenes

- **Design.** After #741. `lens.focusScatter`: the sampled focus error for (seed, shotIndex) is drawn once and moves the
  focus distance every surface's defocus is computed from; `scene.layers` reports each surface's blur at the sampled
  focus and the `lens.focusScatter` assumption is the catalogue's own. `lens.catalogue.breathing`: the lattice's frame
  mapping takes the scale 1 + Δ at the requested focus (every surface's frame point scales by it, as the single plane's
  plane distance does); `scene.layers` gains `breathingScale`, and `scene.framedWidthM` follows.
- **Accuracy and test:** with scatter, each surface's reported blur equals `calculateDefocusCircle` at the sampled focus
  (1e-12) and the render equals a request focused there without scatter, bit for bit. With breathing, a card edge's
  position on the center row moves by the predicted site count within one site; a single card equals the single-plane
  catalogue render with breathing within 1e-9.
- **Fast and registry:** none. **Bundle:** layered chunk. **Files:** `layered-catalogue-kernels.ts` (the sampled focus
  and the scale, as inputs of the kernels and of the geometry's frame scale), tests; the wiring is wave 4's.
  **Size and risk:** S–M; low once #741 is in.

#### #746: camera motion and a rolling shutter on layered scenes

- **Design.** A camera rotation about the entrance pupil has no parallax: frame units are angles, so every surface moves
  together and the rotation is a change of the lattice's direction map at each instant. `framePoint(group, p, q,
  instant)` applies the rotation (the existing `applyPhysicalCameraRotation`) before the frame lookup, and the card
  edges come from the same map (half-planes, #744), so roll is covered. Each temporal midpoint is one layered sum; a
  site's value is the mean over its midpoints. `motion.handheld` is a sampled rotation and takes the same path.
  - **Global shutter:** T sums per frame (8 fast, 16 full, `sensor.temporalSampleCount`), tiles keyed by instant.
  - **Rolling shutter:** each row has its own instants. Full: every row at its own instants. Fast: the existing row
    bands (`motion.shutter.rolling/view-row-bands`: bands that read their middle row's instants, the in-focus image
    shifted by at most 1/128 site), extended to layered views and captures.
  - **Stays refused:** `motion.subject` with a layered scene (which surface moves is not defined by the request); the
    message says so.
- **Accuracy and test.** Zero angular velocity equals the still render bit for bit. A pure yaw of a whole number of
  sites per exposure with one midpoint equals the still render shifted. Global shutter against a brute-force mean of 64
  still renders at 64 instants: within 0.5 % of the local swing. Rolling fast against full: within the row-band bound
  (0.1 code RMS). Capture against view within 1e-9.
- **Cost.** Reported for the tabletop preset with #651's rotation against the still case, and against #711's and #670's
  cases; the estimate (`estimateDisplayViewRender`) counts the midpoints. A request whose work passes the documented
  bound is refused before allocation with the numbers.
- **Fast and registry:** the two existing entries extend their `runs` and text; no new id.
- **Bundle:** layered chunk; wave-4 wiring. **Files:** `layered-lattice-geometry.ts`, `layered-scene-signal.ts` (tiles
  by instant), `layered-scene-provider.ts`, tests. **Size and risk:** L; high (cost under a rolling shutter; last in the
  layered order).

#### #751: faster layered whole-frame captures at large pitch

- **Design.** Profile first (the issue's 192 × 128 tabletop frame, `node --cpu-prof`). The expected cause, to confirm: a
  layered capture runs the environment chain per site through the provider and never takes the tabulated path
  (`tabulatedScene` excludes `layered.multi`), while a single-plane chart does. Candidate: give the tabulated native RAW
  path the layered signal's per-site expected electrons (`createLayeredSceneSignal`, the sum the view reads), checked at
  one site per CFA phase against the environment chain as the tabulated path already checks itself. Capture and view
  already agree within 1e-9, so this is rule 3 (same sum, another route); layered captures' hash locks are re-recorded
  with the equivalence helper.
- **Accuracy and test:** RAW expected values within 1e-9 of the chain on the layered facade tests; samples identical or
  within one code where a draw sits on a rounding boundary (counted and reported).

### Capture path

#### #761 and #762: motion at full quality with the anti-aliasing filter

- **Cause (from the code and the issues' counts).** With camera rotation or a moving subject, `lens.diffraction` is
  applied through the environment PSF quadrature: one scene query per kernel tap. The count per site is temporal
  midpoints × spectral nodes × filter spots × box nodes × taps. The issues' figures factor as 16 × 21 × 4 × 4 × 81 =
  435,456 (#761), × 169 = 908,544 and × 529 = 2,843,904 (#762). The native task admits 100,000 queries per tile, and a
  tile holds at least one site.
- **Design.** Move the tap sum out of the per-site query list and into the scene provider, on the site lattice at each
  instant: the image-space provider (`createDefocusImageSpaceProvider`) already convolves site kernels through the lens
  mapping, rotation and a time-dependent scene (`timesPerBand`, `timeDependentScene`, `bandRows: 1` under a rolling
  shutter). With the diffraction kernels as its site kernels, the environment declares no PSF and a site asks
  16 × 21 × 4 × 4 = 5,376 queries. The full method already reads each tap at its lattice point (#565), so the terms are
  the same; only the order of summation changes.
  - **Bounded memory:** the provider's band height is chosen so that one band's lattice (width + 2R by rows + 2R, per
    node and instant) stays within a byte budget (64 MiB by default, as the spectral tiles), down to one row. The
    limit's purpose is kept: the per-tile query bound is unchanged, and a request whose band cannot fit at one row is
    refused with the numbers.
  - **Where it applies in 2.29.0:** only where the quadrature would pass 100,000 queries per site, which are requests
    refused today, so no request that renders today changes. Making it the one path for every request with rotation and
    diffraction (rule 3) changes last bits and is a follow-up issue.
  - **The message** (#730 `facade-19`): the facade's check becomes unconditional and names the factors.
  - **Catalogue lens with motion and the filter** (a field-varying PSF) is not covered; it keeps the refusal with the
    new message.
  - **#761's second question:** a layered scene with `chartSize` is refused today (`layered-scene-setup.ts`). It is
    accepted in the wave-3 wiring: `chartSize` sets the meters per frame unit at `subjectDistanceM` (u = ±1 at the
    chart's left and right edges), and the far surface continues beyond it, as the frame does today.
- **Accuracy and test.** On requests both paths render (no filter, and the filter at a small window): per-site expected
  electrons within 1e-9 relative, samples within one code. #761's request (1/15 s and 1/60 s) and #762's (f/8 and f/16,
  2 × 2 box, four-spot filter) render at `fast: false`; a region equals the whole frame; peak RSS and CPU time are
  recorded. A moving subject at rest still renders as the still scene (#688).
- **Fast and registry:** none (same sum). **Bundle:** fast row (query planning) and on-demand row (the provider is in
  the CPU Quality kit): both at risk, measured by the group.
- **Files:** `simulate-capture.ts` (wave-2 owner), `defocus-image-space-provider.ts`, `diffraction-provider.ts`, tests.
  The same group takes #730's `facade-12` and `facade-14`, which are in these two providers.
- **Size and risk:** M–L; medium (memory per band at 81 nodes and 16 midpoints; the 1e-9 check decides).

### Processing and measures

#### #740: typical processing stages

- **API.** `simulateTypicalProcessing(input, provider?)`, asynchronous, a thin entry that loads its body on demand (the
  shape of `simulateFalseColor`).

  ```ts
  input: {
    frames: readonly DngRawImage[];            // 1 to 32 identical exposures, as parseDngRawImage or importDngRawImage returns
    develop?: { whiteBalance?, exposurePushStops?, toneCurve?, gamutHandling?, crop?, orientation? }; // developDngImage's
    merge?: false | { method: "mean" };
    noiseReduction?: false | { spatialSigmaPixels?: number; rangeSigma?: number };
    localToneMapping?: false | { contrast?: number; spatialSigmaFraction?: number; rangeSigma?: number; fast?: boolean };
    saturation?: false | { gain?: number };
    format?: "srgb-8" | "srgb-16";
    fast?: boolean;
    maximumWorkingBytes?: number;
  }
  result: { kind: "typical-processing"; label; width; height; format; samples; stages: AppliedStage[];
            fast: { requested, defaultedFrom, applied }; assumptions; provenance }
  ```

  A simulated capture enters as its DNG (`output.format: "dng"`, several `sensor.noiseSeed` values for a burst); an
  imported DNG enters as it is. With one frame and every stage `false` the result equals `developDngImage` bit for bit,
  which is the plain side of a side-by-side comparison. `label` reads "Simulation of typical processing stages; not the
  processing of a specific device".
- **Stages, in this order** (Hasinoff et al., "Burst photography for high dynamic range and low-light imaging on mobile
  cameras", SIGGRAPH Asia 2016, free; Delbracio et al., "Mobile computational photography: a tour", 2021, arXiv):
  1. **Merge:** the mean of the frames' linearized stage images, sample by sample, before the demosaic. Frames must
     agree in size, CFA, levels and color metadata, else the request is refused. No alignment (identical framing); said
     in the assumptions.
  2. **Development** to linear RGB: `developDngImage`'s own chain, split so that its linear image is reachable inside
     the engine (a refactor of `dng-development.ts` with unchanged bytes).
  3. **Noise reduction:** the existing bilateral filter (`createBilateralFilterTask`'s body) on linear RGB.
  4. **Local tone mapping:** the existing Durand–Dorsey operator on linear RGB.
  5. **Saturation** (new): `out = Y + gain·(RGB − Y)` in linear light, Y the luminance of the working space
     (`luminanceWeights`). Analytic per pixel; neutrals and luminance are unchanged exactly.
  6. The tone curve, gamut handling and sRGB encoding of `developDngImage`.
  No stage uses a per-channel lookup table; the tone and saturation steps are closed-form per pixel.
- **Fast and exact.**
  - The demosaic and the WarpRectilinear resampling follow the flag as in `developDngImage` (existing entries).
  - New entry `simulateTypicalProcessing/local-tone-mapping` (override `localToneMapping`): fast is the paper's
    piecewise-linear bilateral with spatial downsampling (today's task); full is the direct bilateral on log luminance
    (window radius ⌈3σ⌉). The registry's `runs` union gains `"simulateTypicalProcessing"`.
  - The direct bilateral's work is pixels × window; it is bounded and checked before allocation, and a request over the
    bound is refused with the numbers and the option that lowers it (decision 9 covers the bound's value).
  - Default: fast for `"srgb-8"` and `"srgb-16"` as everywhere; `fast: false` gives every full method.
- **Accuracy and test.** Merge: on seeded Poisson frames the variance falls as 1/N within 5 % for N = 2, 4, 8, 16 and
  the mean is unchanged within 1e-6. Stages 3 and 4 equal their standalone tasks on the same input bit for bit.
  Fast against full local tone mapping: the difference measured on three scenes and stated as the fast method's bound
  in docs/PROCESSING.md. Saturation: gain 1 is the identity bit for bit, gain 0 gives Y, a neutral pixel is unchanged
  for every gain, and luminance is preserved within 1e-12. Every result is finite; the working-bytes bound holds.
- **Bundle.** A new on-demand chunk with its own measured row (decision 3); it is not in the capture worker's bundle.
  A bundle test fixes the entry's added bytes.
- **Files:** new `src/facade/typical-processing-entry.ts`, `src/facade/typical-processing.ts`,
  `src/processing/frame-merge.ts`, `src/processing/saturation.ts`; `src/capture/dng-development.ts` (the linear seam),
  `src/processing/tone-mapping.ts` (the direct base layer); docs/PROCESSING.md, docs/USAGE.md; tests.
- **Size and risk:** L; medium (the linear seam must not move a byte of `developDngImage`; the full method's cost).

#### #743: false color with noise

- **API.** `simulateFalseColor(options, measure)` gains `measure.noise?: true | { seed?: number }`. Without it the
  result is 2.28.0's, bit for bit. With it the result gains `noise`:
  `{ seed, withNoise: Stats, noiseOnly: Stats, demosaicOnly: Stats }`, each the same statistics block as
  `chromaDifference`, and with `map` or `images` the matching maps and images.
  - `demosaicOnly`: today's measure (noise-free mosaic, demosaiced, against the noise-free full-color reference).
  - `noiseOnly`: a full-color sensor with noise (each site's three planes drawn independently) against the noise-free
    reference: chroma noise without a demosaic.
  - `withNoise`: the capture's noisy mosaic, demosaiced, against the noise-free reference: both together, as a capture
    shows them.
- **Noise.** The mosaic's draws are the capture's own (the same seeded streams and readout chain), so the noisy mosaic
  equals the RAW `simulateCapture` returns for that request and seed. The two extra planes of the full-color reference
  use their own stream ids. Every site's expected value is integrated at the site as today.
- **Accuracy and test.** The noisy mosaic equals `simulateCapture`'s RAW bit for bit. With read noise and shot noise
  turned down by a high exposure, `withNoise` tends to `demosaicOnly` (monotone over three exposures). On a uniform
  patch `demosaicOnly` is zero and `noiseOnly` matches the chroma standard deviation predicted from the per-channel
  variances within 5 % over 4,096 pixels. A region equals the whole frame.
- **Fast and registry:** full quality only, as `simulateFalseColor` (it refuses `fast: true`); no entry.
- **Bundle.** The noise body is a second on-demand chunk, loaded only with `measure.noise`. The existing body row has
  6 B of headroom and the entry delta 52 B: the dispatch goes in the entry if it fits in 52 B, else the group stops and
  reports ([Caps at risk](#caps-at-risk)).
- **Files:** `false-color-entry.ts`, new `false-color-noise.ts`, `false-color.ts` (types only), tests,
  docs/QUICK_START.md ("False color"). **Size and risk:** M; low–medium.

- **Design questions it answers:** the source language and toolchain (built from source in this repository,
  reproducible, license-checked; no third-party binaries); how a module loads in a browser-safe ESM package with no
  runtime dependencies (embedded bytes in an on-demand chunk); the option and environment setting that turn it off and
  their default; the fallback and its report when WebAssembly is blocked (a Content-Security-Policy without
  `wasm-unsafe-eval`); which kernels can never be bit-identical (anything calling `Math.exp`, `Math.pow` or
  trigonometry) and stay in JavaScript; the boundary check's change (`scripts/lib/webassembly-boundary.mjs`).
- **Size and risk:** M; none to the release (documents only).
