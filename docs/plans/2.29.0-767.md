## #767: display view with camera motion or a moving subject, diffraction and the anti-aliasing filter

### Cause (measured)

The display view never asks the environment for one scene query per PSF tap. With a lens map, vignetting, camera
rotation, hand shake or a moving subject, its lens signal (`src/facade/display-view-lens-signal.ts`, `kernelSite`)
already sums each site's kernel taps on the site lattice, per fractional-offset group and per temporal midpoint, from
lattice points kept in a ring. That is the image-space lattice method of #761, summed per site instead of per band.

The refusal comes from one inherited line of `runCapture`. It counts temporal samples × spectral nodes × filter spots ×
pixel-aperture nodes × PSF taps, the capture's old per-tap quadrature, and applies that count to a view only when the
request has the filter:

```ts
if ((antiAliasing || !displayView) && perSite > 1e5) fail("These options need " + perSite + " scene queries per site ...
```

Since #761 the capture leaves the taps out of this count where it sums them on the lattice (`diffractionLattice`); a
view is excluded from that flag (`!displayView &&`), so the view still counts them and refuses.

Probe: with that one term removed (the change below) and nothing else, on six requests of
`test/capture-motion-diffraction-lattice.test.ts` (20 × 14, four-spot filter, 2 × 2 box, `fast: false`: yaw at 1/15 s,
hand shake, a moving subject at f/8 and at f/16 on 4 µm, yaw on dead leaves, rolling yaw at 1/60 s), every third site's
expected electrons from the view's signal against the capture's environment record evaluated with the capture's own
lattice provider:

| Request | Largest relative difference |
| --- | --- |
| yaw, 1/15 s | 2.4e-15 |
| hand shake | 2.4e-15 |
| moving subject, f/8, 4 µm | 3.8e-15 |
| moving subject, f/16, 4 µm | 3.9e-15 |
| yaw, dead leaves | 1.8e-15 |
| rolling yaw, 1/60 s | 2.4e-15 |

### Design

One change of behavior, no new render path:

1. **The capture's lattice flag also holds for a view.** In `simulate-capture.ts`, in the diffraction setup, drop the
   `!displayView &&` term of

   ```ts
   diffractionLattice = !!layered?.multi || !displayView && !!latticeTaps && !defocusKernel && queriesPerSite * (2 * kernelRadiusSamples + 1) ** 2 > 1e5;
   ```

   What follows from it, all existing code:
   - The check above then counts a view as it counts the capture: temporal samples × spectral nodes × filter spots ×
     pixel-aperture nodes, without the diffraction taps. A view with the filter is refused by it exactly where the
     capture is (global shutter); a view without the filter is not checked, as today.
   - The view's `lens.diffraction` assumption reports `execution: "image-space-convolution"`, as the capture's does
     for the same request. Before, a view reported `"environment-psf-quadrature"` for a request whose capture no
     longer takes that path. This also changes that one field (no sample) on views that render today without the
     filter where the tap count passes 100,000, for example rotation with diffraction and a 2 × 2 box at 16 midpoints
     (108,864). It goes in the changelog's migration list.
   - The flag builds nothing for a view: the image-space provider is created on first use by the capture only, and
     the view's kernels come from `diffraction` and `diffractionPsf`, which do not change.
   - A view has no `defocusKernel` in `runCapture`, so the flag also covers motion with `lens.defocus`,
     `lens.diffraction` and the filter, which the capture renders (image-space disc) and the view refused.
2. **Fallback if a hash lock or digest moves because of the assumption field:** keep the flag as it is and leave the
   taps out of a view's count instead (`diffraction && psf === diffractionPsf && !displayView ? ... `). Same refusals,
   same samples, no assumption change; 6 B more on the fast row (measured below).
3. **Nothing changes in** `simulate-display-view.ts`, `display-view.ts`, `display-view-lens-signal.ts`, the kits or
   the GPU code. These requests stay on the CPU reference, labeled `unsupported-on-backend` with `gpu: "auto"`, as
   every view with rotation or a moving subject does today.

Per-site integration (rule 1): unchanged. The ring holds each lattice point's material index (or its spectral radiance
at the nodes) and field factor; the kernels are per node; a site's signal is integrated against its own channel when
the site is read. Nothing is keyed by channel.

**What renders after the change** (probe, a 48 × 32 frame, 24 × 16 view, default fast and `fast: false`): yaw; hand
shake; a moving subject; a panned subject (subject and yaw); rolling yaw; yaw with `lens.defocus`; yaw with AHD; a still
camera through a vignetting profile at f/16 on 4 µm. All were refused before, except the fast moving subject and the
fast vignetting case (under 100,000).

**What stays refused, with the existing texts:**
- A catalogue lens with motion and the filter over the bound (a field-varying PSF; the capture refuses it too, so
  there is no reference to test a view against).
- `motion.subject` with a rolling shutter or a rotating subject (the view's own refusal; not part of #767).
- A count over 100,000 without the taps, where the capture is refused too.

**One case where the view renders and the capture does not:** camera rotation under a rolling shutter on a fast
request. The capture's fast method there is the per-tap one (`lens.diffraction/rolling-shutter-taps`), which is over
the bound and refused; `fast: false` renders. The view has one method under a rolling shutter, the lattice one (each
site at its own instants), so it renders in both modes, as it does today without the filter. The docs say so.

### Fast and full

No new method on either side, so no registry entry (rule 3 does not apply: nothing is replaced).

- **Full (`fast: false`):** the lens signal as it is: 16 midpoints with camera motion, the diffraction window to the
  third dark ring, every display pixel at K × K samples.
- **Fast (the view's default):** the registered fast methods that already list the view in `runs` act on these
  requests as on any other: `sensor.temporalSampleCount` (8 midpoints), `lens.diffraction/kernel-window` (second dark
  ring), `motion.subject/temporal-midpoints`, `view.uniform-pixel-sampling`. Their texts and `runs` do not change.
- Both are built and tested (below).

### Bounds and refusals

- **Refusal text:** no new text. The sentence is the capture's; for a view the last factor is now 1 with
  `lens.diffraction`. Example (128 temporal samples at a 5 nm spectral step), the same for the capture and the view:

  > These options need 165888 scene queries per site (temporal samples × spectral nodes × pupil rays × filter spots ×
  > pixel-aperture nodes × PSF taps: 128 × 81 × 1 × 4 × 4 × 1); the environment admits at most 100000.

  Before, the view of that request on a fast request answered "8128512 ... 128 × 81 × 1 × 4 × 4 × 49".
- **Memory:** unchanged. The lens signal's lattice ring holds at most 2²² entries (about 112 MiB with chart materials:
  28 B per lattice point; about 37 MiB on a radiance scene, where an entry is one spectral value) and its site ring
  2²² values (32 MiB). No band or tile is added. Peak RSS of the probe process stayed between 180 and 430 MiB with and
  without the filter (not an isolated measurement).
- **Work per site:** T × G × taps sums of one value per spectral node (T temporal midpoints, G fractional-offset
  groups: 4 for the 2 × 2 box with or without the default four-spot filter, because the filter's half-site shifts fall
  on the box's lattices). The filter therefore adds no group and almost no work. Measured, a 6000 × 4000 frame on
  6 µm at f/8, a slanted edge, a 32 × 32 region on the edge of a 768 × 512 view, K = 16:

  | Request | With the filter | Without (renders today) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | yaw 0.02 rad/s, 1/15 s, fast | 2.2 s | 2.2 s |
  | the same, `fast: false` | 9.2 s | 6.6 s |
  | moving subject 0.5 m/s, 1/60 s, fast | 6.3 s | not measured |
  | the same, `fast: false` | 22.3 s | 19.3 s |
  | yaw, `fast: false`, AHD | 8.7 s | not measured |
  | yaw, `fast: false`, f/16 on 4 µm (R = 11) | 44.8 s | not measured |
  | rolling yaw, 1/60 s, fast / `fast: false` | 8.7 s / 21.9 s | not measured |

- **Coarse views of a moving subject (owner decision 1).** Where the ring cannot hold one display pixel's window, the
  lens signal reads lattice points as they are needed, without the ring. A moving subject is read as spectral
  radiance, so its ring holds 21 times fewer points than a chart's: with 16 midpoints, 4 groups and 21 nodes it holds
  a window of about 32 × 64 lattice points, a display pixel of about 11 real pixels a side at f/8. Measured, 4 display
  pixels on the edge, K = 16:

  | View of the 6000 × 4000 frame | Request | Time |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 192 wide (31 real pixels a side) | yaw, `fast: false` | 0.5 s |
  | | moving subject, fast | 0.5 s |
  | | moving subject, `fast: false` | 15.6 s (11.7 s without the filter) |
  | 96 wide (62 real pixels a side) | yaw, `fast: false` | 0.8 s |
  | | moving subject, fast | 8.9 s |
  | | moving subject, `fast: false` | 21.9 s |

  This is the lens signal's behavior today for the same requests without the filter; #767 does not add it, but it
  does make more requests reach it. The work stays bounded (at most K² samples × 9 or 25 sites per display pixel, each
  at most T × G × taps × nodes scene values), and it is slow.

Local, no coverage: check 1 took 64 s at every third site in the probe (the reference's environment queries

### Size and risk

S; low.

- **An assumption field changes on some views that render today** (the execution label). Step 2 avoids it at +6 B if
  a lock or a caller depends on it.
- **12 B of room on the fast row.** Step 1 measures −3 B alone; gzip moves by a few bytes with neighboring edits, and
  LW2 changes the same file in the same wave. Integration measures the combination.
- **Slow coarse views of a moving subject at `fast: false`** (decision 1): correct and bounded, but minutes per
  thousand display pixels.
- **The view's reference at high counts is the capture's lattice path,** itself checked against the per-tap quadrature
  only at 2 midpoints (#761). The two were written separately and agree to 4e-15, which is the evidence for both.

### Owner decision

1. **Coarse views of a moving subject with diffraction.** Below about 550 display pixels across a 6000-pixel frame at
   `fast: false` (computed from the ring's size; measured fine at 768 and slow at 192), and somewhere between 192 and
   96 on a fast request (measured at those two), the lattice ring cannot hold a display pixel and the render slows by
   an order of magnitude (table above). The same happens today without the filter.
   - (a) Ship #767 as designed and file a follow-up issue for the ring on radiance scenes, covering requests with and
     without the filter.
   - (b) Also give the radiance ring about the byte budget the chart ring already has, in 2.29.0. One constant in
     `display-view-lens-signal.ts`; no sample changes (the ring is memory only). Measured with a four-times ring: the
     192-wide `fast: false` case falls from 15.6 s to 1.2 s; the 96-wide case does not change (21.9 s). Peak memory of
     a view of a moving subject or another radiance scene through a lens rises from about 37 MiB to about 110 to
     150 MiB for that ring. About +3 B on the fast row (estimate), which leaves no room with step 2.
   - (c) Refuse the newly accepted requests where the ring cannot hold a display pixel, with the numbers, for example
     "simulateDisplayView: this view reads 435456 scene values per site without its lattice cache (16 temporal samples
     × 4 lattice groups × 81 taps × 21 spectral nodes; a display pixel covers 31.25 × 31.25 real pixels and the cache
     holds 32 × 64 lattice points); at most 100000 are admitted. Use a view at least 546 pixels wide, fewer temporal
     samples or fast: true." About +100 B on the fast row (estimate; the lens signal's chunk counts in that row), which
     does not fit without new savings, and requests without the filter would keep rendering slowly.
   - (d) A bounded tile path for wide windows. Probed by reusing the run-convolution tiles with a unit kernel: it
     agrees with the capture (2e-15) but is slower than reading as needed at these scales (31.6 s against 15.6 s, and
     59.7 s against 21.9 s), because it computes every site of each tile for three channels while the view samples
     few of them. A purpose-built path is new code in the fast row.
   - **Recommendation: (a).** #767's acceptance is met without it, the behavior is not new, and the only cheap
     improvement (b) raises the memory of existing requests, which is better decided on its own with a quiet-machine
     measurement.
